Call Earpiece Too Quiet? Causes and Fixes You Can Try at Home

Your music plays fine through the bottom speaker, but during calls the person on the other end sounds distant and muffled. That points at the earpiece, the small speaker at the top of the screen that only plays during calls. It is one of the most common phone audio complaints, and most of the time the cause is simple. Work through these checks in order.

1. Rule out the call itself

Call two different people, or call a voicemail service. If only one contact sounds quiet, the problem is their microphone or signal, not your phone. Also try a call over Wi-Fi calling or an internet calling app to see whether the volume changes.

2. Check the obvious settings

  • Press volume up during a call. Call volume is separate from media volume on most phones.
  • Turn off any hearing or accessibility audio adjustments you have not set up on purpose, such as mono audio or balance sliders.
  • Disconnect Bluetooth. A watch, car or earbuds can quietly take over call audio.

3. Remove the screen protector or case

Thick glass protectors sometimes sit slightly over the earpiece slot, and cases with a raised lip can press against it. Take them off and make a test call. If the sound improves, buy a protector with a proper earpiece cutout.

4. Clean the earpiece grille gently

The earpiece mesh is tiny and collects skin oil, makeup and pocket lint, which act like a plug. With the phone off:

  1. Brush across the slot with a dry, soft toothbrush, moving along the slot rather than into it.
  2. Press a small piece of painter's tape or blue putty against the grille and lift it away to pull out loose debris.
  3. For oily buildup, lightly dampen the brush with a little isopropyl alcohol, brush, and let it dry fully before turning the phone on.

Never push pins, needles or compressed air into the grille. They can tear the mesh or force dirt deeper.

5. Try a vibration cleaning tone

After brushing, a speaker cleaning tone can help shake loose fine dust and moisture from the earpiece membrane. A free call speaker cleaner plays tones designed for this small speaker; hold the phone with the earpiece facing down so loosened particles can fall out, and run it for a minute or two at moderate volume.

6. Test after a restart and update

Restart the phone and install pending system updates. Audio glitches after updates do happen, and a restart clears temporary software problems that can mute or limit the earpiece.

When it is time for a repair

If the earpiece crackles, cuts in and out, or stays quiet after cleaning and software checks, the speaker itself may be damaged, often after a drop or water exposure. Earpiece replacement is a routine repair, so ask an authorised service centre for a quote before considering a new phone.
